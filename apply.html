Come to a day out

This is the easy bit.

You are asking for an invitation to a day out. That is all this is — a few families, somewhere good, a few hours. There is nothing to sign and nothing to pay to find out whether you like us.

If you have never met a group like this before, the first day is the awkward one. Everybody knows that. Somebody will come and find you, introduce you around, and it stops being awkward in about ten minutes. Your children will beat you to it.

Worth knowing before you come

How our days tend to feel.

Friendly, easy, and slightly chaotic in the good way. Nobody performs, nobody is assessed, and the adults get to have a proper conversation for once — which for most of us is the bit we did not know we were missing.

What everybody brings

  • Good energy, and a hand with whatever needs doing
  • Curiosity — happy to hear an idea you have not heard before, and to offer one back
  • Kindness towards every child there, not only your own
  • Food you would be glad for any of the children to see you eating
  • Your own mess, taken home with you

What we leave at home

  • Smoking or vaping anywhere near the children
  • Fizzy drinks, sweets, and the beige-and-orange end of the menu
  • Talking down to children — ours or anybody else's
  • Turning up to be entertained and waiting for somebody else to run the day

This is not a purity test. Nobody here has it worked out and nobody is keeping score. We are all just trying to be a bit better than we were last year.

The reason it matters at all is that at this age children copy what they see far more than what they are told. So the standard is about our own example, never about judging each other's.

And bring your laptop if you need to. If a work call lands halfway through, take it — your children will be busy. The whole point of this is that providing for them and being with them stop having to be two separate lives.

A sentence is plenty, and there is no right answer — we just like knowing who we are talking to before we meet you. Skip it if you would rather.

What happens now

  1. We read what you sent — usually within a week.
  2. You get an invitation to the next day out near you.
  3. You come along, meet everybody, and see how your children take to it.
  4. If you love it, you keep coming. That is the whole commitment.

And this is what we believe

  • The family is the centre. Everything else is supplemental, including us.
  • Providing and being present should not be two separate lives. That is the problem we are actually solving.
  • Children learn by doing difficult things near people who like them. Not by being tested.
  • Asking for help is a skill, not a weakness. We teach it on purpose.
  • We are open to the best way. No method owns us. What holds up, stays.

One thing to try this week.

Give your child a goal instead of an instruction. Not "put the blocks away" but "see if you can get every block into that box before I finish this cup of tea." Then say nothing and watch what they do. That gap — where you want to help and you do not — is where the whole thing lives.
