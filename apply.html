How we do it

This is the part that makes us different.

We give children a goal and no method.

"Build a remote control car. Now make it get all the way round a loop." Nobody hands them the instructions. They try it, it fails, they change something, it works.

They can ask each other, ask an adult, ask an AI. In most classrooms that is called cheating. Here it is called working — because no adult is ever asked to solve a real problem alone with no help.

The one thing we cannot bend on

Here, you stay.

We do not take your child off your hands.

You are in the building. Working in the parent work zone, watching, or joining in — whichever suits the day. Your child can find you in twenty steps whenever they want you.

And you stay responsible for them, exactly as you would be at a park or a soft play. That is not a technicality, it is the entire design. It is the reason this place exists.

What happens now

  1. We read your application properly — usually within a week.
  2. If it looks like a fit, you get an invitation to the next day out.
  3. You come along, meet everybody, and watch your child in the middle of it.
  4. You decide. Nothing is signed before any of that.

While you wait, this is what we believe

  • The family is the centre. Everything else is supplemental, including us.
  • Children learn by doing difficult things in front of people who like them. Not by being tested.
  • Asking for help is a skill, not a weakness. We teach it on purpose.
  • A calm room beats a clever curriculum. Tone first, always.
  • We are open to the best way. No method owns us. What holds up, stays.

One thing to try this week.

Give your child a goal instead of an instruction. Not "put the blocks away" but "see if you can get every block into that box before I finish this cup of tea." Then say nothing and watch what they do. That gap — where you want to help and you do not — is where the whole thing lives.
